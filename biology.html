Key Terms You MUST Know First:

🧬 Allele: A single version of a gene. Here we have G (dominant, green) and g (recessive, gold).

🧩 Genotype: The combination of two alleles inside an individual (GG, Gg, or gg).

🎨 Phenotype: The physical appearance (Green or Gold).

🌿 The Hardy-Weinberg Principle is like a mathematical mirror. It tells us what happens to allele frequencies in a population if there is NO EVOLUTION (no selection, no mutations, random mating, infinite size, and no migration). In this ideal state, the alleles stay perfectly constant!

The Math Decoded

The Two Golden Equations

To solve any Hardy-Weinberg problem, you only need to master two basic formulas.

Equation 1: Allele Frequencies
p + q = 1

• p = Frequency of the Dominant allele (e.g., Green allele G)

• q = Frequency of the Recessive allele (e.g., Gold allele g)

💡 Real-world tip: If 60% of all alleles are G (p = 0.60), then 40% must be g (q = 0.40) because 0.60 + 0.40 = 1.

Equation 2: Individual / Genotype Frequencies
p² + 2pq + q² = 1

• p² = Frequency of Homozygous Dominant individuals (GG)

• 2pq = Frequency of Heterozygous individuals (Gg)

• q² = Frequency of Homozygous Recessive individuals (gg)

💡 Real-world tip: In a study, you can count physical recessive individuals (gg) easily because they look different. Therefore, you always start solving from q²!

Instructor Tips for Success

💡

Identify Phenotypes First

Always look for words like "recessive trait", "recessive disease", or "white/blonde/short". These always represent $q^2$.

⚠️

Don't Mix Up Alleles with Individuals

Allele frequency queries want $p$ or $q$. Individual frequency queries want $p^2$, $2pq$, or $q^2$. If they want individual COUNT, multiply the frequency by total population.

🔍

Check Your Sums

Ensure $p + q$ always equals exactly $1.0$. If they do not, you made an arithmetic error.
