Core Highlights from Chapter: Population Genetics

Review critical scientific concepts compiled directly from the lesson text.

Learning Objectives

  • Define population genetics and describe how scientists track evolutionary change in populations.
  • Understand the Hardy-Weinberg Principle of Equilibrium as an experimental control.
  • Calculate mathematical changes in allele and genotype frequencies ($p + q = 1$ and $p^2 + 2pq + q^2 = 1$).
  • Differentiate evolutionary forces: mutation, genetic drift, gene flow, nonrandom mating, and natural selection.

Key Vocabulary Terms

Allele Frequency

The rate at which a specific allele appears within a population's gene pool.

Modern Synthesis

Coherent integration of Darwinian natural selection and Mendelian genetics (by the 1940s).

Genetic Drift

Changes in allele frequencies due solely to random chance, magnifying effects in small populations.

Gene Flow

Flow of alleles in/out of a population due to migration of individuals or gametes.

Adaptive Evolution

Natural selection favoring beneficial alleles while acting against deleterious ones.

Polymorphism

Expression of multiple different phenotypes / alleles of a gene within a population.

Natural Selection Styles

Stabilizing

Favors intermediate, average phenotypes. Example: Robin clutch size (typically 4 eggs).

Directional

Favors one extreme phenotype. Example: Industrial Revolution peppered moths (shifting light to dark).

Diversifying

Favors two or more extreme phenotypes, selecting against average. Example: Sneaker/Alpha males.

Classic Case Studies Mentioned

  • 🧬 Side-Blotched Lizards (Rock-Paper-Scissors): Orange (aggressors) beats Blue (pair-bonders), Blue beats Yellow (sneakers), Yellow beats Orange. Creates a stable cyclical pattern of phenotypic frequencies.
  • 🏜️ The Bottleneck Bead Game: Simulated natural disasters showing that survivors are random samplings. Genetic diversity changes drastically without directional evolutionary purpose.
  • ❄️ Geographic Clines: Altitudinal (mountain flowering schedules) and Latitudinal (larger body sizes of homeothermic species near the colder poles).
